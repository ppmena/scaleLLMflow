# Minimal MQS test article

## Objective

This pilot randomized controlled trial evaluated a six-week online training
program for 100 primary-care professionals. The inclusion criteria were at
least one year of clinical experience and regular internet access. Professionals
without internet access were excluded.

## Participants and attrition

One hundred professionals were randomized: 50 to the intervention group and 50
to the control group. At post-test, 48 intervention participants and 45 control
participants remained. At the three-month follow-up, 46 intervention and 42
control participants remained. The study reported the numbers lost from each
group but did not report any imputation method for missing data.

## Design and follow-up

The study used a parallel-group randomized controlled design. Outcomes were
measured at baseline, immediately after training, and three months later.

## Control techniques

The control group received an alternative online training program. Allocation
was random, and outcome assessors were blinded to group assignment. Inter-rater
reliability was assessed by double-coding 20% of interviews.

## Outcomes

The primary outcome was clinician confidence in delivering the intervention,
measured with a named 10-item questionnaire. The questionnaire score and its
interpretation were defined in the Methods section.

## Analysis

The analysis used independent-samples tests and linear regression. No method
for imputing missing observations was described.

## Conclusion

The intervention group showed higher confidence at post-test and follow-up.
